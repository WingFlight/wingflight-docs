    Tune Advisor Internals¶

    How the Tune Advisor works, end to end: what the flight controller measures, how the radio stores and combines it, the exact rules that produce the advice, and exactly which parameters Save writes. For using it, see Tune Advisor.

    The work is split in two:

    Side Does Source
    Flight controller Measures how the model answers the rate loop and reports the numbers over MSP. Gives no advice and changes no settings. src/main/flight/tune_advisor.c, MSP2_WING_TUNE_ADVISOR in src/main/msp/msp.c (wingflight-firmware)
    Radio (Ethos Lua suite) Reads the numbers after each flight, keeps the last 5 flights, works out the advice, and writes it to the flight controller when the pilot presses Save. tasks/tune_history.lua, lib/tune_history.lua, lib/msp_tune_advisor.lua, app/pages/tune_advisor.lua (wingflight-lua-ethos-suite)

    The advice rules live on the radio so they can change without flashing the flight controller.

    Firmware: what is measured¶

    The feature is built when USE_TUNE_ADVISOR is defined, which common_pre.h does on boards with more than 128 KB of flash. tuneAdvisorUpdate() runs every PID loop, after the PID controller.

    Sampling¶

    PID loops are averaged into one sample every 10 ms (100 Hz). Each sample holds, per axis (roll, pitch, yaw):

    Value Taken from
    Setpoint (deg/s) The PID controller's setpoint
    Gyro (deg/s) The PID controller's gyro rate
    I-term The PID controller's I output, in surface units
    PID sum The PID controller's full output, in surface units (1 = full travel)
    Stick deflection -1 to 1
    Saturated Set if the mixer saturated that axis in any loop of the block

    plus the throttle (0 to 1).

    Which samples count¶

    A sample is valid only if, in every PID loop of its block:

    • the model is armed and airborne, and
    • none of ANGLE, ATT HOLD, TRAINER, GPS rescue, LOITER, RTH, failsafe, PASSTHROUGH or MANUAL is active.

    On top of that, the body must have been rotating: |roll| + |pitch| rate of at least 40 deg/s within the last 200 samples (2 s). Yaw is left out, so taxi turns do not count as flying; on real wing logs they lowered the roll correlation. This also keeps armed stick checks on the ground out.

    Invalid samples are not used for anything below. The reported flight time is the number of valid samples / 100, in seconds.

    Response (feed-forward match)¶

    F sends the setpoint straight to the surfaces, so the ratio gyro / setpoint says how well F fits the airframe: 1.0 means the model flies the rate it is asked for; above 1 it turns faster than asked (F too high for the rates).

    The model answers late (servos, aerodynamics), so the setpoint is compared delayed by 0 to 25 samples (0 to 250 ms). For each delay the firmware keeps three running sums over usable pairs: Σ setpoint·gyro, Σ setpoint², Σ gyro². A pair is usable when:

    • the current sample is valid and the axis is not saturated now, and
    • the delayed setpoint came from a valid, unsaturated sample with 40 ≤ |setpoint| < 200 deg/s.

    From these, for each delay:

    • gain = Σ setpoint·gyro / Σ setpoint² (least-squares slope through zero)
    • correlation = Σ setpoint·gyro / √(Σ setpoint² · Σ gyro²)

    Once a second the delay with the highest correlation becomes the best lag. The reported ffGain, ffCorr and ffCount are those of the best lag, and ffLagMs is the lag × 10 ms.

    At the best lag the same ratio is also split into bands:

    Bands Split by Edges
    Setpoint bands Size of the request, |setpoint| 40–100, 100–200, 200+ deg/s
    Throttle bands Throttle below 30%, 30–60%, 60% and above (only requests below 200 deg/s)

    Throttle matters because prop wash raises surface authority and TPA does not scale F.

    Full stick¶

    A sample counts as full stick when the stick deflection one best-lag ago was at least 90%. For those samples the firmware counts how many there were (fullCount) and how many were saturated (fullSatCount), and keeps the rate reached in the stick's direction against the rate asked:

    • fullRatio = Σ rate reached / Σ |setpoint|
    • fullMaxRate = the highest rate reached (deg/s)

    Saturated samples are included here: this is what tells the radio the surfaces ran out of travel.

    Stops (stick releases)¶

    Per axis, a small state machine follows each stick input:

    1. Input: starts when |setpoint| leaves the centre band (5 deg/s). While the stick is out, the setpoint and gyro extremes are kept.
    2. Release: when the setpoint returns inside 5 deg/s. The direction of the input is the side with the larger setpoint peak. At that moment the I-term is recorded, signed so that positive means opposing the input that just ended.
    3. Watch: for the next 80 samples (0.8 s), the largest rate in the opposite direction is the rebound, and the largest opposite PID sum is the counter-surface. The rate peak in the input direction keeps updating for the first 10 samples, as the model coasts.
    4. The release counts only if the input asked for at least 60 deg/s and the model reached at least 60 deg/s. If the stick moves out again within 15 samples (0.15 s), it is not a stop and is dropped; after that, a new input ends the watch early and the release still counts.

    For each counted release the firmware adds up:

    Value Meaning
    releases Number of counted releases
    bigRebounds Releases with a rebound of 15% of the peak rate or more
    meanRebound Mean of rebound / peak rate
    meanOvershoot Mean of peak rate / peak setpoint
    meanCounter Mean peak counter-surface (PID sum) after the release
    meanIterm Mean I-term at the release, positive = pushing back against the input

    A small counter-surface with the I-term near zero points at the airframe rebounding on its own (not enough damping). A positive I-term points at the I-term pushing the model back.

    Accumulation and reset¶

    Everything accumulates in RAM across flights until it is reset. It is not saved and is lost at power-off. It is reset when:

    • MSP2_WING_TUNE_ADVISOR_CLEAR is received (the radio sends it after saving each flight), or
    • the model arms on a different tune. At each arm the firmware hashes the current PID and rate profile numbers, every PID gain (P, I, D, F, B, O) of the PID profile, Master Gain, I-term Relax, PID mode, and the RC rates, expo and super rates of all axes. If the hash differs from the previous arm, the statistics start again.

    The tune is only checked at arming. A setting changed in flight (for example by an adjustment function) is not noticed until the next arm.

    MSP interface¶

    Command ID Direction Payload
    MSP2_WING_TUNE_ADVISOR 0x5F18 Read Request: U8 axis (0 roll, 1 pitch, 2 yaw). Any other length or axis is an error.
    MSP2_WING_TUNE_ADVISOR_CLEAR 0x5F19 Write Empty. Resets all statistics.

    One axis per request keeps the reply (65 bytes) small enough for MSP over telemetry. Firmware without the feature answers with an MSP error.

    Reply, payload version 1. Ratios are signed 16-bit × 1000; counts saturate at 65535; all values little-endian.

    Offset Type Field
    0 U8 Payload version (1)
    1 U8 Collecting now (1 = the current sample is valid)
    2 U16 Seconds of valid flight
    4 U8 Axis
    5 U16 P gain (current PID profile)
    7 U16 F gain
    9 U16 B gain
    11 U8 I-term Relax score for this axis (1–10)
    12 U8 RC rate for this axis (raw; the Rates page shows raw × 5 deg/s)
    13 U16 ffCount
    15 S16 ffGain × 1000
    17 S16 ffCorr × 1000
    19 U16 ffLagMs
    21 3 × (S16, U16) Setpoint bands 40–100, 100–200, 200+: gain × 1000, count
    33 3 × (S16, U16) Throttle bands <30%, 30–60%, 60%+: gain × 1000, count
    45 U16 fullCount
    47 U16 fullSatCount
    49 S16 fullRatio × 1000
    51 U16 fullMaxRate (deg/s)
    53 U16 releases
    55 U16 bigRebounds
    57 S16 meanRebound × 1000
    59 S16 meanOvershoot × 1000
    61 S16 meanCounter × 1000
    63 S16 meanIterm × 1000

    The tune values at offsets 5–12 are the settings the numbers belong to, so a client can work out new values without reading the profiles first. The radio refuses a reply of any other length or version rather than guess at it.

    Radio: capturing each flight¶

    The background task captures every flight, whether or not the Tune Advisor page is open.

    On each disarm:

    1. Wait 5 s (the flight-stats sync goes first).
    2. Read roll, then pitch, then yaw, with 2 s between one answer and the next request. Over S.Port and FBUS each reply takes about 15 telemetry frames, so the gaps keep sensors updating.
    3. If a read fails, ask again for that axis only after 5 s; axes already read are kept. Each read is sent at most 3 times. After 3 link errors the capture stops until the next disarm. The flight controller still holds the numbers, so that disarm saves them. If the flight controller answers with an MSP error (firmware without the Tune Advisor), the capture stops until the next disarm.
    4. If the three replies report different flight times, the flight controller restarted or flew between reads, and the capture starts again.
    5. Save the three axes as one flight, unless the flight time is 0 (no valid flight) or the same as the last flight saved for this model (the previous clear never arrived and nothing new was flown).
    6. Send MSP2_WING_TUNE_ADVISOR_CLEAR, so the next flight is measured on its own.

    Arming during a capture abandons it. The flight controller was not cleared, so on the same tune the next disarm saves both flights as one; on a changed tune the firmware resets at arming and the earlier flight is lost. A disarm during a link loss is captured on reconnect, as long as the flight controller was not powered off.

    The page itself asks the flight controller for one axis when it opens, only to learn whether the firmware has the Tune Advisor; an MSP error shows Needs newer firmware.

    Files on the radio¶

    All under LOGS:/wfsuite/tune/<mcuId>/, where <mcuId> is the flight controller's ID (the same one that names the flight log folders):

    File Contents
    history.csv One row per axis per flight, newest last. Only the last 5 flights are kept. Columns: date, flight_seconds, axis, then every reply field in the order above (p, f, b, relax, rc_rate, ff_count, …, thr0_gain, …, mean_iterm). A file with a different header is started again.
    logs.ini The model name.
    changes.csv Every value Save wrote: date,axis,setting,from,to, raw values as the flight controller stores them. Appended, never trimmed, never erased by the page.

    Combining flights¶

    For each axis separately, the page starts from the newest flight and goes back up to 5 flights, stopping at the first flight whose tune on that axis differs from the newest one. The tune is: P, F, B, Relax and RC rate for that axis.

    • Counts are added (ffCount, band counts, fullCount, fullSatCount, releases, bigRebounds, seconds).
    • ffGain, ffCorr and ffLagMs are averaged weighted by ffCount; each band's gain by its count; fullRatio by fullCount; the four stop means by releases.
    • fullMaxRate is the highest of the flights.

    The firmware's gains are weighted by setpoint² within a flight, so the combined response is a close estimate. The stop figures combine exactly.

    Only those five values decide which flights are combined. A change to I, D, Master Gain or expo resets the flight controller's measurements at the next arm, but flights before and after it are still combined on the radio.

    Radio: the advice rules¶

    All rules work per axis, on the combined numbers. At most 3 changes and 3 reasons are shown; a change and its reason are added together or not at all.

    Thresholds¶

    Name Value Used for
    Response data needed ffCount ≥ 1000 (10 s) Judging the response at all
    Consistent enough ffCorr ≥ 0.85 Judging the response at all
    Too fast / too slow ffGain > 1.15 / < 0.85 F and RC Rate change
    F step limit ±20% per step F change
    Full stick data needed fullCount ≥ 100 Full-stick RC Rate change
    Surfaces at their limit fullSatCount ≥ 50% of fullCount Full-stick RC Rate change
    Rate not reached fullMaxRate < 80% of the rate asked Full-stick RC Rate change
    Stops needed releases ≥ 10 Judging stops at all
    Bounce too big meanRebound ≥ 0.12 Stop changes
    I-term pushing back meanIterm ≥ 0.03 Relax change
    Throttle fact both outer bands ≥ 300 samples, spread ≥ 25% Information only

    Response¶

    Checked in this order:

    1. ffCount < 1000: Needs more flying (shown as a percentage of 1000). The page asks for rolls, pitch inputs or rudder inputs. No change.
    2. ffCorr < 0.85: Too uneven to judge. No change. Common on rudder.

    3. ffGain above 1.15 or below 0.85: change F and RC Rate together:

      • new F = round(F × clamp(1 / ffGain, 0.8, 1.2)), limited to 50–1000 (50 is the firmware's lowest F: MANUAL flies on F)
      • new RC Rate = round(RC Rate × old F / new F), limited to 1–200

      F × rate is what sets how far the stick moves the surfaces, so that stays the same; the setpoint now matches what the model does. This applies to yaw too. 4. Otherwise the response matches. Then, on roll only, if the full-stick conditions above all hold, RC Rate is set to what the model reaches: new RC Rate = round(fullMaxRate / 5), limited to 1–200. The rate asked is RC Rate × 5 deg/s. Pitch is left out because 3D flying parks at full elevator on purpose.

    Stops¶

    1. releases < 10: Needs more stops. No change.
    2. meanRebound < 0.12: shown as the bounce-back percentage. No change.
    3. meanRebound ≥ 0.12, first matching case:
      • I-term pushing back (meanIterm ≥ 0.03), Relax below 10, and room for a change: new Relax = Relax + 1. A higher Relax score lowers the I-term relax cutoff (scores 1–10 are 50, 30, 20, 15, 10, 8, 7, 6, 5 and 3 Hz), so the I-term builds less during the input.
      • The response is still off (step 3 above applies): reason only, "fix F first".
      • Otherwise, with room for a change: new P = round(P × 1.2), limited to (P + 1) … 1000. The page also mentions B as an alternative but never changes B.

    F with RC Rate is two changes, so a Relax change can still be added next to them.

    Throttle fact¶

    If the response is judged and both the low and high throttle bands have at least 300 samples, the larger band gain / smaller band gain is computed. At 1.25 or more the page adds a reason such as "It turns 30% faster at high throttle than at low". This is information only and is the first reason dropped when there is no room.

    Radio: what Save writes¶

    Save writes the changes for the axis on screen only. These are the only parameters it can ever change:

    Setting (as the page names it) CLI parameter MSP message, field Accepted range
    PIDs > axis > P roll_p_gain, pitch_p_gain, yaw_p_gain MSP_SET_PID_TUNING (202), <axis>_p 0–1000
    PIDs > axis > F roll_f_gain, pitch_f_gain, yaw_f_gain MSP_SET_PID_TUNING (202), <axis>_f 50–1000
    Flight Feel > axis > Relax iterm_relax (one value per axis) MSP_SET_PID_PROFILE (95), I-term Relax for the axis 1–10
    Rates > axis > RC Rate roll_rc_rate, pitch_rc_rate, yaw_rc_rate MSP_SET_RC_TUNING (204), rcRates_<1-3> 1–200

    All of them belong to the active PID or rate profile. Save never changes I, D, B, Master Gain, expo, super rate, filters or any other setting, and never touches another axis or profile.

    The write sequence¶

    1. Confirm. The dialog lists every change exactly as the page shows it.
    2. Read MSP_PID_TUNING (112), MSP_PID_PROFILE (94) and MSP_RC_TUNING (111). A failed read, or a reply too short to hold every field, stops here with nothing changed.
    3. Check, and stop with nothing changed if any of these fail:
      • the model is disarmed;
      • the active PID and rate profile are the same as when Save was pressed;
      • P, F, B, Relax and RC Rate on that axis are exactly the values of the newest flight the advice came from (so a hand edit, a profile switch or advice already applied stops it);
      • every value about to change still holds its expected old value, and every new value is in range.
    4. Write only the messages that contain a change, in the order PID tuning, PID profile, RC tuning. Each is a full-message write: every other field is sent back exactly as it was read in step 2.
    5. Save with MSP_EEPROM_WRITE (250).
    6. Verify: read all three messages again and check each changed field holds its new value.
    7. Append each change to changes.csv.

    If a write fails, nothing has been saved to EEPROM, and restarting the flight controller undoes any part already sent. If the EEPROM save fails, the changes are active until the next restart.

    The saved flights are kept. Because the tune changed, the next flight starts a new combination on the page, and the firmware resets its statistics when it next arms.

    Clearing¶

    The page's Tool button deletes history.csv for the model and sends MSP2_WING_TUNE_ADVISOR_CLEAR. changes.csv and logs.ini are kept.
